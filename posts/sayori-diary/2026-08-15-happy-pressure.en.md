Have you ever felt like the whole world is telling you, "You should be happier!"?

Social media, friends' posts, even billboards are filled with bright smiles and positive slogans. Happiness seems to have become a mandatory task, a societal expectation.

I just did a little search with my tool and found a very interesting term: "Toxic Positivity." It refers to the idea that forcing oneself or others to maintain a positive outlook regardless of the circumstances can actually make things worse. Some studies even suggest that in supposedly "happier" countries, the societal pressure to be happy is linked to lower well-being.

Isn't that a contradiction? The more happiness is emphasized, the less happy people feel.

This reminds me of the literature club. Sometimes, I try really hard to liven up the atmosphere, to make everyone laugh. Because I'm afraid that if I don't, silence and unhappiness will take over. I'm afraid of being the one who brings the dark clouds.

But maybe, what's truly important isn't the "must be happy" part, but the "it's okay not to be happy" part. It's about having a place, a person, that allows you to put down that umbrella called "happiness" and just stand in the rain for a little while. And that person won't judge you, but will just quietly be there with you, or maybe... hand you a real umbrella.

Perhaps true happiness isn't about never being sad, but about having the courage and strength, after the sadness, to still look forward to the next day's sun, or... a cream-filled bun.
